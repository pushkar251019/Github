How to sweep a room

No single phone test catches everything. Work through the tools in order. Each one catches a different kind of device:

  1. Magnetic: finds powered electronics and batteries hidden in walls, smoke detectors, chargers and so on.
  2. Lens finder: finds pinhole camera lenses by the light they reflect back.
  3. IR / night-vision: shows infrared LEDs on cameras that film in the dark.
  4. Bluetooth: lists nearby BLE devices and flags trackers (AirTag, Tile, SmartTag) and unnamed devices.
  5. RF buzz: listens for the 217 Hz "GSM buzz" that SIM-card bugs make, and for ultrasonic beacons.
  6. Checklist: a physical search of the places devices are usually hidden.

Device support

Honest limits

A phone has no wideband radio receiver, so no app can directly detect every wireless microphone, especially analog VHF/UHF bugs or devices that record and send nothing. This app uses the sensors your phone has to raise the odds of finding them. For a full radio sweep, plug a ~$30 RTL-SDR dongle into a laptop and scan 24 MHz–1.7 GHz with free tools (SDR#, SDR++, or rtl_power).

If you think you're being watched by someone who could harm you, don't remove the device yourself. Contact the police or a support service first, because removing it can warn the person.

Magnetic / electronics detector

Calibrate away from metal, then move the top of your phone slowly (5–10 cm away) over outlets, vents, clocks, smoke detectors, USB chargers, lamps and furniture. A sharp spike where nothing obvious should be is worth a closer look.

Pinhole camera lens finder

Turn off the room lights. Start the scan (the flashlight turns on if your phone allows it, otherwise hold a second torch next to the camera). Sweep slowly. Camera lenses reflect light straight back as a small, sharp, steady glint, often blue or purple. Red circles mark candidate spots. Move a little: a real lens stays bright, a random reflection flickers away.

Infrared / night-vision camera check

Cameras that film in the dark light the room with infrared LEDs your eyes can't see, but many phone cameras can. Test first: point a TV remote at the camera and press a button. If you see the LED flash, that camera sees IR. Front cameras often see it better than rear ones.

Then, in a dark room, sweep the room. Glowing purple or white dots, often in a ring, mean IR LEDs.

Bluetooth device & tracker scan

Lists nearby Bluetooth Low Energy devices with signal strength. It flags trackers (AirTag/Find My, Tile, Samsung SmartTag, Chipolo) and devices that hide their name. Walk around: a stronger signal (closer to 0 dBm) means you're getting closer.

Also check

iPhone: Settings → Privacy & Security → Safety Check, and Find My → Items → "Identify Found Item". iOS warns you about unknown AirTags travelling with you.

Android: Settings → Safety & emergency → Unknown tracker alerts → Scan now.

Wi-Fi cameras: use the free Fing app on the room's Wi-Fi and look for devices named like "IPCam", "Espressif", "HiSilicon" or unknown makers.

RF buzz & ultrasonic listener

GSM/SIM-card bugs make a "dit-dit-dit" buzz at 217 Hz in nearby speakers and microphones when they transmit. Some trackers use ultrasonic beacons (18–22 kHz). Hold the phone near suspicious objects in a quiet room. To provoke a bug, call or text the room's number, or stay quiet for a while then talk, since many bugs are voice-activated.
